# NUMI — Project Brief

&gt; One page. If it can't survive being one page, it isn't locked yet.

---

## The problem, in my own words

Money leaves my hands in small amounts — R50s, R100s — and I don't notice it going. I only understand what happened after it's gone, as regret: *that could have been used for something.* This isn't unique to me. It's true in my family too. We earn money. We still can't manage it, not because we don't care, but because we can't **see** it clearly enough, in the moment, to choose differently.

This is not a discipline problem. It's not that I need to be stopped from spending. It's that the information arrives too late to be useful.

## Who this is for, specifically

Me, first. My family, second. Then — people who live the same pattern: irregular or unpredictable income, spending that happens in small increments that don't feel like "real" transactions until they add up, and no existing tool built for that reality.

This pattern is confirmed by an external, independent source: the "Managing Money — Skills for Success" guide (see `docs/resources/money_habits_for_study_success.md`), a nationally distributed financial literacy handbook for South African students. It explicitly teaches the same techniques NUMI automates — dividing lump-sum income across categories, tracking planned vs. actual spending, and calculating a daily safe-to-spend number. The fact that this is taught as a standard lesson proves the pattern is widespread, not personal.

## What NUMI actually does

NUMI is a financial clarity app designed to answer one simple question: **Will my money last?**

Instead of only showing where your money went, NUMI helps you see where it's going, gives every Rand a purpose, and guides you toward better financial decisions — without guilt or complexity.

Three things follow from that:

1. **Capture must be frictionless.** Money moves in R50s. Logging it can't take longer than spending it.
2. **The view must be *now*, not later.** Not a monthly report. A number I can see before I hand over cash.
3. **Set-aside money must feel *spoken-for*, not spendable.** Not locked away. Not hidden. Just clearly labeled as already committed, so my hand pauses before it moves.

NUMI shows money through three lenses — the same three lenses taught in standard South African financial literacy education:

- **Current** — What do I have right now that is truly free to spend?
- **Planned** — What have I already committed this money to, and will it last?
- **Actual** — What really happened, so I can learn without shame?

## What "done" means for v1

Someone in a position like mine can:

- Enter money in and money out in under 5 seconds, without navigating menus
- See, at a glance, what is safe to spend *right now* without breaking commitments already made
- Set money aside for a specific purpose and have it read as *unavailable* for casual spending
- Use the app fully without an internet connection, account, or subscription

**Explicitly not v1:** goals, debt tracking, CSV import, cloud sync, voice logging, widgets. These are specified in the Playbook and scheduled for later phases.

## What this is not

- **Not an automation tool.** It doesn't decide for me. I still choose — it just makes the choice visible in time to matter. (Agency, not automation.)
- **Not a discipline/lockout tool.** No restrictions, no shame, no blocking a purchase.
- **Not built for someone who already has visibility and wants optimization.** That's Revolut, Monarch, Copilot's market. Not this one.

## The one sentence, locked

&gt; **NUMI exists to make invisible spending visible in the moment — free for everyone, with those who can afford more funding those who can't.**
