# Source: Managing Money — Skills for Success

## Why this document matters to NUMI

This is a government-aligned, mass-distributed financial literacy guide given to South African high school students. It teaches the exact patterns NUMI is built to solve:

- Lump-sum income (bursaries, allowances, part-time work) that must be stretched across time
- The need to divide money into categories before spending
- The emotional difficulty of sticking to a plan when peers and stress exert pressure
- The gap between *planning* money and *tracking* what actually happens

## Key Concepts Relevant to NUMI

### 1. The Three-Lens View (Current / Planned / Actual)

The source material explicitly uses a budget planner structured around three columns:

| Category | Current | Planned | Actual |
|---|---|---|---|
| Income | R | R | R |
| Basic needs | R | R | R |
| Financial protection | R | R | R |
| Debt repayment | R | R | R |
| Savings | R | R | R |
| Lifestyle spending | R | R | R |
| **Surplus or shortfall** | R | R | R |

**What this means for NUMI:** The "Current / Planned / Actual" model is not a NUMI invention. It is the established pedagogical standard for South African financial literacy. NUMI digitizes and automates this exact worksheet.

- **Current:** What do I have right now?
- **Planned:** What have I committed this money to?
- **Actual:** What really happened?

### 2. The "Structure Your Income Smartly" Framework

The source presents a honeycomb model for dividing income:

- **Share:** Family, community
- **Spend:** Needs (rent, food, electricity, data) and Wants (holiday, eating out, sneakers)
- **Protect:** Emergency savings, medical cover, insurance
- **Build:** Short-term goals, medium-term goals, long-term goals, retirement savings
- **Behaviour:** Start good money habits now

**What this means for NUMI:** The app must allow users to allocate money into these mental buckets. The "Spend" vs "Build" tension is central — money set aside for goals must feel *gone* from daily spending.

### 3. The Specific User Pattern

&gt; "As you start out, you may feel you don't yet have enough money to be able to choose how you manage it. But it's never too soon. Learning to manage what you have now will benefit you for the rest of your life."

&gt; "Write down your total definite monthly income... List your expenses... track your spending... compare your planned spending to your actual spending."

&gt; "Work out how much you have to spend on basics and entertainment every day (divide the money you have in your budget for the month by the number of days in the month)."

**What this means for NUMI:** The target user is someone who believes they don't have "enough money to manage." NUMI must feel useful even with small amounts (R50s, R100s). The daily-safe-to-spend number is explicitly taught as a standard technique.

### 4. Money and Emotions

&gt; "When you feel stressed or tired, you might be tempted to spend on unplanned items... Or when you feel pressure to impress or fit in with your friends..."

&gt; "Understanding the principles of money isn't difficult, but consistently doing what you know you should can be trickier. Often, this is about how you manage your emotions and the impulses they create."

**What this means for NUMI:** The app is not an accounting tool. It is an emotional-regulation tool. Frictionless logging and visible remaining balances are designed to interrupt the impulse-spending cycle.

### 5. Offline-First Necessity

The source warns extensively about digital scams, phishing, and the dangers of public WiFi for banking. It also notes that many students operate with basic phones and limited data.

**What this means for NUMI:** The offline-first architecture is not just a technical preference. It is a safety and accessibility requirement for the target demographic.

### 6. Compound Interest and Time

The source heavily emphasizes starting early, even with small amounts. The Qondiswa vs. Hlomla example (starting at 18 vs. 28) demonstrates that time matters more than amount.

**What this means for NUMI:** The app should celebrate small wins. Saving R50 is not trivial — it is the beginning of a habit that the source proves is life-changing.
